Journal / 07

Useful before
the project starts

Short, practical answers to questions that often come up before an interior project begins.

Planning · 6 min

When should an interior designer get involved?

While installations, lighting and the room layout are still open for discussion.

Read the guide ↘

The right time to involve an interior designer is before installations, suspended ceilings and flooring are built. The layout can then be checked without returning to work that is already finished.

For a new build, a reliable architectural floor plan is enough to begin. For a renovation, the design should start before individual contractors are booked. Movement, function, furniture positions and lighting come first. Materials follow.

An early design leaves less room for improvisation on site. Wall colour can wait. The socket behind a wardrobe cannot.

Budget · 8 min

What actually belongs in an interior budget?

A price per square metre rarely tells the whole story. The real scope does.

Read the guide ↘

The budget covers construction and trade work, finishes, sanitaryware, lighting, appliances, loose furniture, textiles, custom furniture, delivery and installation. Renovations also need a contingency for what only becomes visible after walls or floors are opened.

Permanent elements that work hard every day come first. Kitchens, wardrobes, floors, worktops and hardware need more careful choices than decoration that can be replaced later.

A good cost plan connects prices with lead times and the sequence of work. Decisions are made before the site has to wait for them.

Custom furniture · 7 min

A custom kitchen: decisions before the first drawing

A good kitchen begins with how it will be used, the appliance list and the position of installations. Fronts come later.

Read the guide ↘

Before drawing begins, it helps to know who uses the kitchen, how often cooking happens, which appliances are needed and what must be stored. Only then can work zones, circulation and tall units be sized properly.

Appliance dimensions should be confirmed before production begins. Door openings, ventilation, service access, socket positions and wall tolerances matter too. The front material has to suit daily use and maintenance.

When design and production are connected, details are resolved before installation. There is less improvisation and the furniture fits the space more accurately.

FAQ / 09

Before the first
conversation

When is the right time to involve a designer?

Before final decisions are made about installations, lighting, flooring and joinery. Starting early leaves more room for a well-resolved layout and reduces costly changes later.

How long does a project take?

Timing depends on the size and condition of the space, the scope of service and the amount of custom furniture. A realistic schedule is set after reviewing the floor plan and project brief.

How is the budget defined?

The estimate covers work, equipment, lighting, furniture, delivery, installation and contingency. A single price per square metre is not reliable because every space has a different scope.

Can I commission only part of the service?

Yes. The available options are Interior Concept, Interior + Custom Furniture, and Complete Interior with coordination and delivery.

What should I send before the first conversation?

A floor plan or measured sketch, photos of the space, location, approximate floor area, target schedule, a short description of needs and an initial budget range.

Is custom furniture included?

Yes. Furniture is developed as part of the interior, including materials, hardware, joints, lighting and installation.

Are projects outside Zagreb possible?

Yes. Site visits, measurements and coordination are planned according to location and project type.

Are hospitality and B2B projects available?

Yes. The service is available for boutique hotels, villas, restaurants, offices and developer projects.
